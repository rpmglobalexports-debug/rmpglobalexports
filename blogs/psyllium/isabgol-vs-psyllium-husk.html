Isabgol vs Psyllium Husk — Same Product, Different Names

You're searching for a psyllium husk supplier. Your Indian contact calls it "isabgol." The pharmacopoeia says "ispaghula husk." A European buyer might write "Plantago ovata husk." These are all the same product — just different names used in different markets and regulatory traditions.

The Short Answer

Isabgol = psyllium husk. The word "isabgol" is the Hindi and Gujarati name for the outer seed coat of Plantago ovata — the same crop that international trade calls "psyllium husk." The underlying commodity, the botanical origin, the regulatory category, and the HS classification are identical regardless of which name appears on the invoice.

Where confusion arises is not between "isabgol" and "psyllium husk" but within the product family itself — between the seed, the husk, and the powder, which are three distinct forms with different applications and specifications.

Where the Name "Isabgol" Comes From

The word "isabgol" has Persian roots — derived from "isap ghul," meaning "horse ear," a reference to the shape of the plant's leaves. In Gujarat, where nearly all commercial cultivation occurs, the crop has been called isabgol for generations. In Hindi, the husk form is commonly called isabgol ki chilka (isabgol's peel or husk). In Gujarati, it is simply isabgol.

The English trade name "psyllium" comes from the Greek word for flea — a reference to the small, dark seed. "Ispaghula" — the form used in British pharmacopoeias and regulatory frameworks in the UK, Australia, and parts of the EU — is an Anglo-Indian derivation of the same word. The World Health Organisation monograph uses "ispaghula," while the US FDA and American food industry predominantly use "psyllium husk." All refer to exactly the same product from Plantago ovata.

Why Both Names Appear in Trade Searches

This naming divergence creates real ambiguity when international buyers search for suppliers:

  • An Indian supplier in Unjha will naturally describe themselves as an "isabgol exporter" — that is what the product has always been called in their market.
  • A buyer in the USA or Canada typically searches "psyllium husk exporter from India."
  • A UK or EU regulatory buyer might search "ispaghula husk supplier."
  • A Middle Eastern bulk buyer may search "isabgol wholesale" or "isabgol bulk price."

The result: buyers and suppliers are often looking for the same thing using completely different search terms, and the search results reflect each group's terminology rather than a single definitive product name. If you find a supplier listing for "isabgol exporter India" — that is a psyllium husk exporter. No translation needed.

Grade Terminology: Kha-Kha, Husk, and Powder

This is the terminology that genuinely requires clarification for international buyers, because it represents three distinct products — not just naming variations for the same one:

Kha-Kha (also: khakha, isabgol bij, psyllium seeds): The whole, intact seed of Plantago ovata — not processed into husk. "Kha-kha" is the regional Gujarati trade name; internationally it appears as "psyllium seeds," "isabgol seeds," or "plantago ovata seeds." This is the raw seed before the outer coat is separated. It is not psyllium husk, and its applications are different. See our psyllium kha-kha product page for specifications and uses.

Psyllium Husk (isabgol chilka / isabgol husk): The outer seed coat separated from the seed through milling and classification. This is the high-fibre, gel-forming material most international buyers want — the standard "psyllium husk" of pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, and food trade. It is sold at purity grades of 98% and 99%, with the higher purity grades commanding a premium and used in regulated pharmaceutical applications.

Psyllium Husk Powder: Finely milled psyllium husk, used in food formulations where a smooth, dispersible texture is needed — protein shakes, gluten-free baking blends, and functional food products. The powder form may carry a different sub-classification in some destination countries' tariff schedules, so verify with your customs broker if you're importing powder rather than whole husk.

What to Specify on a Purchase Order

To be unambiguous with an Indian supplier — and to avoid receiving the wrong form — specify the following when requesting a quotation:

  • Product form: Psyllium Husk (not seed, not powder — state the form explicitly)
  • Purity grade: 98% minimum (food/supplement grade) or 99% minimum (pharmaceutical grade)
  • Swelling index / swelling volume: State your minimum requirement if applicable to your end-use (pharmaceutical buyers in particular will have a specified minimum)
  • Moisture limit: Typically stated as 8% maximum
  • HS code reference: 1211.90.32 (India export) / 1211.90 (6-digit international) — so there's no ambiguity on the commercial invoice
  • End-use context: Pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, or food — this tells the supplier which quality standard (BP/EP/USP specifications or food-grade) your CoA needs to be prepared against

Acceptable alternative names on the invoice: Psyllium Husk, Isabgol Husk, Ispaghula Husk, Psyllium Seed Husk. All of these refer to the same product and will be accepted interchangeably by most customs authorities.

Regulatory Name by Market

As a quick reference for buyers in different markets:

  • USA / Canada: Psyllium husk (FDA, Health Canada)
  • European Union: Ispaghula husk (EFSA / pharmacopoeia standard) or psyllium husk (food supplement context)
  • UK: Ispaghula husk (MHRA / British Pharmacopoeia)
  • Australia / New Zealand: Psyllium husk (TGA / FSANZ)
  • India (domestic): Isabgol
  • WHO Monograph: Ispaghula

Using the term your regulatory authority recognises on import documentation minimises the risk of customs queries, particularly for pharmaceutical-grade imports.
