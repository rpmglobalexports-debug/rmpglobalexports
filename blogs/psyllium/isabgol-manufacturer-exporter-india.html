Isabgol and Psyllium Husk: The Same Product

If you've seen both "isabgol" and "psyllium husk" in supplier listings and wondered whether they're different products, they're not. Isabgol is the Hindi and Gujarati name for psyllium husk — the outer seed coat (husk) of the Plantago ovata plant. In international trade, the same material is also called ispaghula husk (the pharmacopoeia name used in British and European regulatory contexts).

All three terms — isabgol, psyllium husk, ispaghula husk — appear on different documents for the same product. The Indian export community predominantly uses "isabgol" in domestic trade communications, while shipping documents and international purchase orders use "psyllium husk." When evaluating a supplier, make sure the product specification, not just the name, matches what you need. Our isabgol vs psyllium husk guide covers the terminology in more detail.

Where Isabgol Is Manufactured in India

India accounts for a significant share of the world's psyllium husk supply, with production concentrated in Gujarat. The primary growing regions are Unjha, Sidhpur, Mehsana, Banaskantha, and Patan — all within Gujarat state. Most processing and cleaning of raw isabgol seed into exportable husk or powder happens in or around these belts.

Unjha APMC (the agricultural produce market committee) serves as the central daily trading market where isabgol prices are discovered. Most Indian exporters base their quotations on Unjha APMC rates adjusted by grade, processing costs, and logistical factors. Shipments typically move through Mundra or Kandla ports in Gujarat.

Manufacturer vs. Exporter: What's the Difference?

These terms are sometimes used interchangeably in Indian trade, but they describe different roles:

  • Manufacturer — processes raw isabgol seed or partially processed material into cleaned, graded husk or husk powder. Manufacturers operate processing lines, cleaning equipment, and packing facilities.
  • Exporter — handles international trade logistics: shipment documentation, customs clearance, booking sea freight, and ensuring the product is packed to export standards.
  • Manufacturer-exporter — the most common structure in Gujarat's isabgol trade. Many suppliers handle both their own processing and direct export, which typically means cleaner traceability from raw material to final shipment.
  • Merchant exporter — buys finished isabgol from processors and sells for export without owning processing facilities. Pricing can be competitive, and a well-connected merchant exporter often has access to multiple processing units to source specific grades.

Neither structure is inherently superior — what matters is whether the supplier can document the product's quality, origin, and processing with verifiable certificates.

Certifications to Check Before Placing an Order

Any serious isabgol manufacturer or exporter from India should be able to share the following registration documents on request:

IEC — Import Export Code

Issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT). Mandatory for all Indian exporters. Without an active IEC, a supplier cannot legally export. This is a basic verification step that often gets skipped.

FSSAI Central License

Issued by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. A Central License (as opposed to a State License) is required for businesses involved in food export. Check that the license is currently active — it has a validity date and must be renewed annually.

APEDA RCMC — for Psyllium Husk Specifically

APEDA (Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) issues export registration certificates called RCMC (Registration Cum Membership Certificate). For psyllium husk / isabgol, the relevant panel under APEDA is Herbal and Medicinal Plants.

Important: APEDA RCMC is product-specific. A supplier may hold APEDA registration for one commodity but not for isabgol. Always confirm the certificate scope covers psyllium husk specifically. RPM Global Exports holds APEDA RCMC for psyllium husk — you can verify on our certifications page.

GST Registration

A GSTIN confirms the business is registered under India's Goods and Services Tax system and is operating as a verified legal entity.

Processing Standards: What to Look For

The isabgol manufacturing process significantly affects the final product's quality and consistency. Key processing factors to ask about:

  • Cleaning and sorting — good quality isabgol husk is cleaned to remove seed coats, foreign matter, and discolored material. Ask specifically what foreign matter levels the supplier achieves.
  • Mesh grading — particle size consistency matters for applications like capsule filling or sachet mixes. Confirm the mesh specification and how it's measured and controlled.
  • Moisture management — isabgol is hygroscopic and absorbs moisture if not stored and processed correctly. The specification should state a maximum moisture limit; typical acceptable values are listed on our product page.
  • Batch traceability — a manufacturer who cannot tell you which crop lot or APMC arrival corresponds to a given batch cannot support a genuine recall or quality investigation.

Product Specifications: Confirmed Benchmarks

When evaluating an isabgol manufacturer's product specifications, the following parameters are most commonly referenced by international buyers:

  • Purity — Food grade: ≥95%. Pharma grade: ≥98–99%. These figures should appear on the supplier's Certificate of Analysis.
  • Swelling index — Measures water-absorption capacity in ml/g. Food grade typically ≥45 ml/g; pharma grade ≥60–70 ml/g. Higher is generally better for supplement and pharmaceutical applications.
  • Moisture content — ≤10% for food grade; ≤8% for pharma grade is a common threshold.
  • Dietary fiber content — ≥80% (food grade), ≥85% (pharma grade) is a standard benchmark.
  • Foreign matter — ≤1% is a typical commercial specification.

A supplier should be able to back every one of these claims with a third-party Certificate of Analysis — not in-house test results. The difference matters because an in-house CoA can be produced for any figure; a third-party NABL-accredited lab report is independently verified.

What a Third-Party NABL CoA Means

NABL is India's National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories. A NABL-accredited lab meets international ISO/IEC standards for testing competence. When an isabgol supplier provides a CoA from a NABL-accredited lab, it means the test results were produced by an independent, accredited facility — not self-reported.

This matters most for buyers in regulated markets (EU, US, Canada, Australia) where product documentation is subject to scrutiny by customs, food safety authorities, or downstream customers. Before placing a bulk order, verify that the lab name on the CoA is actually NABL-accredited — it's possible to look up labs on the NABL website.

Export Documentation for Isabgol

A complete isabgol export shipment should include:

  • Commercial invoice — stating HSN code, unit price, quantity, and value
  • Packing list — bag count, gross and net weights, marks and numbers
  • Bill of lading — issued by the shipping line at the port of loading
  • Certificate of origin — confirms the product originates from India; often required by destination customs for duty purposes
  • Certificate of Analysis — from a third-party NABL lab, covering purity, moisture, swelling index, and other buyer-specified parameters
  • Phytosanitary certificate — issued by India's plant protection authority; required by most importing countries for agricultural product shipments
  • Fumigation certificate — where required by the destination country's biosecurity authority

The HSN code for isabgol on Indian export documents is 12119032. See our HS code guide for how this maps to import classifications in the USA, EU, and other markets.

Packaging Options from Indian Exporters

Standard export packaging for isabgol is 25 kg multiwall paper or PP bags. Many exporters also offer 50 kg bags. For buyers requiring private-label packaging — your own brand or label on the bag — this is available on request from exporters who have the packaging setup to accommodate it, though it typically requires a minimum quantity to be commercially viable. Ask during the enquiry stage, not after the order is placed.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is isabgol the same as psyllium husk?

Yes. Isabgol is the Hindi/Gujarati trade name for psyllium husk (Plantago ovata husk). "Ispaghula husk" is the pharmacopoeia name. All three refer to the same product. Read our full explainer on the terminology.

2. What certification should an isabgol exporter from India hold?

At minimum: IEC (export licence), FSSAI Central License (food safety), GST registration, and APEDA RCMC for psyllium husk. All four should be active and verifiable on request.

3. What is the difference between a manufacturer and an exporter for isabgol?

A manufacturer processes raw isabgol into husk or powder. An exporter handles international shipment and documentation. Many Indian suppliers are manufacturer-exporters — they do both. Merchant exporters source finished product and handle the export side.

4. What is the MOQ for isabgol from India?

5. Where is isabgol manufactured in India?

Primarily in Gujarat — particularly Unjha, Sidhpur, Mehsana, Banaskantha, and Patan. Unjha APMC is the main daily market for price discovery and trading.

6. What HSN code is used for isabgol exports from India?

HSN code 12119032. This code appears on all Indian export shipping bills and commercial invoices. At the international 6-digit HS level: 1211.90.

7. What documents should an isabgol exporter provide?
