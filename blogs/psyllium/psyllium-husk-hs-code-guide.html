Psyllium Husk HS Code 1211.90.32: A Classification Guide for Importers

If you're importing psyllium husk from India, the HS code on your commercial invoice affects more than paperwork — it determines your duty rate, your eligibility for FTA benefits, and whether a phytosanitary certificate is required. Here's what to know before the shipment is booked.

What Is HS Code 1211.90.32?

The Harmonised System (HS) code for psyllium husk under India's export classification is 12119032. At the 6-digit international level this is 1211.90 — part of Heading 1211, which covers "plants and parts of plants used primarily in pharmacy, perfumery, or for insecticidal or similar purposes — other."

Breaking it down by chapter: Chapter 12 covers oilseeds, industrial crops, and medicinal plants. Heading 1211 is where psyllium sits — a medicinal plant product with established pharmaceutical and nutraceutical applications. The "1211.90" subheading is the "other" category within that heading; the India-specific 8-digit extension 12119032 narrows it further to psyllium specifically.

When your shipment arrives at the destination port, customs authorities will typically use either the 6-digit international prefix (1211.90) or their own country-specific tariff schedule extension. The 6-digit code is the global standard; the additional digits vary by country.

How Classification Affects Your Import Costs

Three things most importers care about are directly tied to HS classification:

  • Import duty rate. The applicable tariff for psyllium husk varies significantly by destination market — the EU, USA, UAE, Canada, and Australia each apply different rates under their respective tariff schedules. The HS code is the key your customs authority uses to look up that rate.
  • Free Trade Agreement eligibility. Several of India's active FTAs (including India-UAE CEPA, India-ASEAN FTA, and India-Mauritius CECPA) offer preferential duty rates on specific HS codes for Indian agricultural exports. These agreements are indexed by HS code — correct classification is a prerequisite for claiming any preferential rate. An incorrect or vague classification forfeits the benefit.
  • RoDTEP rebate eligibility in India. Under India's Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products scheme, specific HS codes attract different rebate rates for the exporting supplier. Correct classification ensures your supplier can claim applicable rebates, which can influence the final quoted price.

What Documents Carry This HS Code

For a standard psyllium husk shipment from India, the following documents will reference or be directly linked to HS code 12119032:

  • Commercial Invoice: Must specify the HS code alongside product description, quantity, and FOB or CIF value. This is the primary document destination customs references.
  • Packing List: Accompanies the invoice; the HS code is sometimes included here as well.
  • Bill of Lading (sea) / Airway Bill (air): Carries product description and country of origin. Destination customs uses this alongside the invoice to verify classification.
  • Phytosanitary Certificate: Required for all plant-derived products, including psyllium husk, entering most international markets. Issued by India's NPPO (National Plant Protection Organisation) through the plant quarantine authority. The commodity and its classification appear on this certificate.
  • Certificate of Origin: Required if you're claiming preferential duty under an FTA. Issued by a recognised chamber of commerce or — for eligible APEDA-registered exporters — through APEDA's certification pathway.
  • Certificate of Analysis (CoA): Not an HS-code document per se, but frequently required by destination customs for pharmaceutical or nutraceutical-grade psyllium imports. Third-party NABL-accredited lab CoAs carry more weight than in-house test reports.

Common Classification Errors and How to Avoid Them

Three mistakes appear regularly in psyllium husk import shipments:

Using 1211.90.90 ("other medicinal plants") instead of 1211.90.32. Some traders declare psyllium under the general "other" sub-subheading rather than the specific psyllium code. This can trigger customs queries, flag the shipment for additional inspection, or — if the destination country's tariff applies a lower rate specifically to the psyllium subheading — result in paying more duty than necessary.

Confusing psyllium husk with psyllium seed. The whole seed — sometimes called kha-kha or isabgol bij — and the husk are distinct products, though they often share the same HS heading at the 6-digit level. If you're importing seed rather than husk, verify the applicable sub-code in your country's tariff schedule. See our psyllium kha-kha product page for the distinction between seed and husk forms.

Misclassifying psyllium as a food ingredient under Chapter 19 or 21. Psyllium husk is not a food preparation — it is a medicinal plant product classified under Chapter 12. Declarations under the wrong chapter can result in customs rejection or a mismatch that delays clearance.

What to Confirm with Your Indian Supplier Before Booking

Four questions worth settling before the container is loaded:

  1. What HS code will you declare on the commercial invoice? It should be 12119032 for psyllium husk — not a generic "other plants" code. Ask them to confirm in writing.
  2. Can you provide a Phytosanitary Certificate? A legitimate APEDA-registered exporter with an active IEC can obtain this through plant quarantine authorities. Confirm before shipping — some buyers find out mid-shipment that their supplier couldn't arrange one.
  3. Is a Certificate of Origin needed for my market? If you're in a country with an active FTA with India, a proper CO is the paper trail that gets you the preferential rate. Your supplier needs to know which FTA applies and what certifying body to use.
  4. What CoA format does my customs authority require? Pharmaceutical and nutraceutical buyers especially: the EU and USA often require third-party NABL-accredited lab CoAs rather than in-house test reports. Confirm the format before the product is packed.

Isabgol, Ispaghula, Psyllium — One HS Code

The same product is sold internationally under several names — "isabgol" (Hindi/Gujarati), "ispaghula" (pharmacopoeia/UK), and "psyllium husk" (US/international food trade). All refer to the outer seed coat of Plantago ovata and all classify under HS 1211.90 / 12119032. The name variation does not affect the HS classification, but it is worth checking that your commercial invoice uses the term your customs authority recognises — particularly if you're importing for regulated pharmaceutical or food applications.
